A framework, not a fixed curriculum

AI is a dynamic subject. The syllabus should act like one.

This map covers KG through graduation, built so a class can start one way and end somewhere completely different — on purpose.

A class might begin by building a classroom AI "buddy" — and end in a full debate about whether a robot could ever replace a teacher. That's not a derailment. That's the class working. The KG–7 program below is a network of entry points, not a lesson-by-lesson schedule, so that kind of turn is something to follow, not something to fight.
Primary focus

KG–7: eight entry points

Start wherever the room's energy is. Each card shows where it can naturally lead — click to jump there.

Meet Your AI Buddy

Opens the year, or resets any class that needs one. The group names a classroom AI "buddy" and asks it questions together.

  • K–2 whole class, teacher types, pure wonder
  • 3–5 pairs ask their own questions
  • 6–7 groups try to stump it, report back
Leads to: Can It Be Wrong?Give It a VoiceBig Questions

Give It a Voice

Make the AI talk like a pirate, a dinosaur, a tired superhero. A first look at how framing changes an answer.

  • K–2 whole class shouts ideas for one persona
  • 3–5 small groups build their own
  • 6–7 two personas answer the same question, side by side
Leads to: Class CreatureCan It Be Wrong?

AI Music Makers

New this year. The group describes a feeling or an animal and listens to what the AI invents.

  • K–2 three words, one class listen
  • 3–5 a theme song for a made-up animal
  • 6–7 compare two descriptions, two results
Leads to: Class CreatureCan It Be Wrong?

Can the Robot Be Wrong?

Ask for a cat with five legs. Laugh. Then wonder out loud why it happened — no stress attached.

  • K–2 a funny mistake, as a class
  • 3–5 a spot-the-mistake game
  • 6–7 try to trick it, then discuss why
Leads to: Big QuestionsAI Helps People

Our Class Creature Collection

Every student contributes one idea; the AI brings the whole class's creature to life. This year's version of the comic-book project.

  • K–2 one word each, one shared creature
  • 3–5 pairs design their own
  • 6–7 individual creature + a two-sentence field guide entry
Leads to: Give It a VoiceShowcase Day

AI Helps People

A translation, a read-aloud, a described photo. Where AI quietly does something kind — plants "tool, not toy" without a lecture.

  • K–2 watch a phrase get translated
  • 3–5 try read-aloud or image description
  • 6–7 brainstorm who this could help, and how
Leads to: Big QuestionsShowcase Day

Big Questions

Could a robot be a teacher? Is it cheating to use AI on homework? No right answer here — just the argument, and that's the point.

  • K–2 robot teacher or human teacher? thumbs up, one reason why
  • 3–5 two sides debate, then switch
  • 6–7 a real mini-debate — the bridge to Course 2's AI Debate unit
Can lead anywhere — often where a class actually ends.

Showcase Day

Share one thing made this term — a creature, a song, a conversation — with another class, parents, or each other. No grades.

  • All bands same format, scaled by what was made
Often spawns its own Big Questions — a fine way for a term to end.

New to the room, or want a default? Buddy → Voice → Music → Can It Be Wrong → Creature → Helps People → Big Questions → Showcase works as a spine. Just don't hold it once the class pulls somewhere else.
